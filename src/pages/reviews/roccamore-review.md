Most brands claiming comfort have added padding and hoped. Roccamore did something more specific: it built its insole with a Copenhagen orthopaedic shoemaker, designed to hold the foot closer to anatomically correct while it is pitched forward inside a heel. That's a structural claim rather than a marketing one, and it deserves to be assessed as such.

**How we assess.** This review draws on Roccamore's published design rationale and construction details and the recurring themes in publicly available customer reviews. We have not run a controlled wear test and do not claim to. See our [affiliate disclosure](/affiliate-disclosure) for how the site is funded.

## Verdict in Brief

**Rating: 4 / 5**

The comfort engineering is genuine and it is the reason to buy. The deductions are for a narrow style range and a bonded sole that caps how long the shoe lasts.

**Buy it if** comfort is a physical requirement — standing work, long events, or a history of pain in heels.

**Skip it if** you want a wide choice of silhouettes, need a wide fit specifically, or want a shoe you can resole.

## What the Insole Actually Does

The claim is narrow and therefore credible: the insole is shaped to support the arch and distribute load while the foot is angled forward, developed with an orthopaedic specialist rather than a product designer.

What that means in practice is that Roccamore addresses the *fatigue* dimension of heel discomfort — the slow accumulation over hours of standing — more directly than anything else in our directory. What it does not address is *pinching*, which is a function of the last and the toe box. Those are different problems and the distinction matters when deciding whether this brand solves yours.

The aesthetic deserves credit too. Nothing about a Roccamore reads as an orthopaedic shoe. It looks like a clean Danish heel, which is precisely why it works as a recommendation.

## Where It Falls Short

**The range is narrow.** This is the direct consequence of building everything around one insole — the shapes are consistent and fairly classic. If you want a specific silhouette, [Flattered](/brands/flattered) makes considerably more of them at a similar price.

**Bonded soles cap the lifespan.** Not resoleable, so four to seven years of regular wear is the realistic horizon. At $200–$350 that is defensible, but it is not an investment purchase, and it is worth knowing before you frame it as one.

**It doesn't solve width.** Standard last. Buyers with genuinely wide feet sometimes arrive here expecting a fit solution and find a fatigue solution instead. Our [wide-fit edit](/edit/best-scandinavian-heels-wide-feet) covers the brands that actually address that.

## Sizing

True to EU sizing, standard width, limited half sizes. One of the safer blind orders in the directory — measure properly using the [sizing guide](/guides/scandinavian-shoe-sizing) and order your measured size.

## Who Should Buy It

Roccamore is the clearest recommendation in our directory for one specific person: someone who wants to wear heels, has a physical reason not to, and has been told the options are ugly. It is not the most versatile brand, not the longest-lasting, and not the cheapest. It is the one that solves that problem.

For everyone whose requirement is "shouldn't hurt by dinner" rather than "must correct my posture", [Flattered achieves that for less](/edit/roccamore-vs-flattered), with far more choice of shape.
